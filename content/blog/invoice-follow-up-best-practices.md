# 5 Invoice Follow-Up Best Practices That Actually Get You Paid

Late payments are a reality of business, but how you handle them makes all the difference. Here are five proven strategies for following up on invoices while maintaining great client relationships.

## 1. Set Clear Payment Terms Upfront

Prevention is better than cure. Before you even send an invoice:

- **Be explicit**: State payment terms in your contract
- **Include due dates**: Always specify when payment is expected
- **Outline late fees**: If you charge them, make it clear
- **Offer payment options**: Make it easy for clients to pay

## 2. Send Timely Reminders

Don't wait until an invoice is overdue to remind clients:

### Recommended Timeline:
- **7 days before due**: Friendly reminder
- **On due date**: Payment due notification
- **7 days after due**: First overdue notice
- **14 days after due**: Second overdue notice
- **30 days after due**: Final notice

## 3. Keep It Professional and Friendly

Your tone matters. Here's what works:

**Do:**
- Use a warm, professional tone
- Reference the specific invoice number
- Include a direct payment link
- Express appreciation for their business

**Don't:**
- Be aggressive or threatening
- Assume the worst
- Make it personal
- Forget to include payment details

## 4. Make Payment Easy

Remove friction from the payment process:

- Provide multiple payment methods
- Include a direct "Pay Now" button
- Ensure invoices are mobile-friendly
- Accept partial payments when appropriate

## 5. Know When to Escalate

If friendly reminders aren't working:

1. **Call directly**: Sometimes email gets lost
2. **Offer payment plans**: Be flexible when possible
3. **Involve leadership**: Copy their accounts payable
4. **Document everything**: Keep records of all communication
5. **Consider collections**: As a last resort
